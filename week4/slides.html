Today in five lines

Five things to remember (X = cause, Y = outcome)

1

Selecting on X doesn't bias the estimate.

Only some values of the cause (X) get into the sample. It just limits who the results apply to.

2

Selecting on Y biases the estimate, usually towards a weaker relationship.

Only some values of the outcome (Y) get into the sample, so there is less variation in Y to explain.

3

Random error in X makes the relationship look weaker than it really is.

Noise in the measure of the cause blurs its link to the outcome.

4

Random error in Y doesn't bias the estimate. It just makes it less precise.

The data is messier, but the slope is still right on average.

5

Non-random error can push the estimate either way.

The mistakes are linked to the other variable: ask what the mistake is linked to.

Also: validity = measuring the right thing. Reliability = same answer each time.

Our estimate vs the truth: flatter or steeper?

Each box in a column means the same thing. Green dashed = the truth. Orange = our estimate.

When the true effect is negative

Over/under = how strong the effect looks. Up/down = what the number does. With a negative truth they point opposite ways.

Definitions

Selection: who ends up in the data

Selecting on X

Only people with certain values of X (the cause) end up in the data.

Example: we only survey students who study 20 or more hours a week.

Selecting on Y

Only people with certain values of Y (the outcome) end up in the data.

Example: we only survey students who failed the exam.

Definitions

Random error: mistakes in measuring

Random error in X

X (the cause) is measured with mistakes, sometimes too high and sometimes too low, by chance.

Example: students estimate their weekly study hours from memory: some guess too high, some too low.

Random error in Y

Y (the outcome) is measured with mistakes, sometimes too high and sometimes too low, by chance.

Example: exam marks are typed in with slips: some are a few marks too high, some too low.

Collider bias

A false pattern made by who gets through

A collider is something that is caused by two different things. If we only look at cases where the collider has a certain value, the two causes can look linked even when they are not.

Everything on one page

Operationalising: concept (the idea) versus measure (what we record).

Direction words: flatter line = weaker = UNDER = closer to zero. Steeper = stronger = OVER = further from zero.

Random error: in X (talent rating) the line gets flatter. In Y (success) it just gets messier.

Selection: on X, less range. On Y, a flatter line. Ask who is missing and why.

Collider: selecting on a shared result creates a false link.

Wrap up

Four things to take away

1

Measures are not the idea.

Operationalising turns an idea into something we can record. Ask how close the measure is to the idea.

2

Random error in X flattens the line.

Error in the talent rating gives a flatter line: UNDERestimate. Error in success only adds scatter. Systematic error: reason out the direction.

3

Selection: ask who is missing and why.

Selecting on Y flattens the line. Selecting on X narrows the range. Selecting on a shared result can create a false link.

4

Colliders: a shared result.

Selecting on it, or controlling for it, can make two unrelated things look linked.
